"Found in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts Better via Plug-and-Play Positional Encoding"

Standard RoPE causes attention weights to decay with distance — tokens in the middle of long inputs get "lost." Ms-PoE assigns different scaling ratios to different attention heads: some heads keep the original scale (preserving local precision), while others use rescaled positions to maintain long-range reach. This is training-free and plug-and-play.
pos'(i, head_h) = pos(i) × scale_h
scale_h ∈ {1.0, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1} per head
What does the scale value mean?
RoPE encodes positions by rotating vectors. The further apart two tokens are, the more their rotations diverge, causing attention to decay.

Scale = 1.0 (original): Full rotation speed. Positions 10 and 200 look very different to the math → attention drops sharply with distance. Great for local word-order precision, but "loses" middle tokens.

Scale = 0.1 (rescaled): Rotation speed is 10× slower. Positions 10 and 200 now look almost identical → attention barely decays → the head can "reach" token 1,000 with the same strength as token 10. This is "distant reach".

"Near-uniform attention" means the head looks at the entire document equally, finding broad concepts rather than specific local phrasing. The trade-off: it loses fine word-order precision.

⚠ RoPE Attention Decay — Why Ms-PoE is Needed
Standard RoPE (scale=1.0) causes exponential decay with distance. Rescaled heads (lower scale values) flatten the decay curve, allowing the model to attend to distant tokens.

Method Properties

❌
KV Cache: Adjusts attention weights but still stores every token individually — memory grows linearly.
❌
Quadratic Scaling: No compression; still compares every word to every past word.
✅
Immediate Context: No compression used — exact wording perfectly preserved.
✅
Long-Term Amnesia: All data retained in KV cache without deletion or blurring.
✅
Lost in Middle: Rescaling position indices across heads prevents attention decay mid-document.
